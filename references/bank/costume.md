# Bank: Costume, Motif, Gravity, Texture, Hair

Layers 2–3 of the design stack. It is a source book, not a parts catalogue. A seed is one short metaphor, not a finished costume; if a candidate already specifies sleeves, filigree, and tassels, it is a purchased outfit — useful as a texture reference, useless as a motif.

Register definitions are in [../contract/gates.md](../contract/gates.md) §2; the no-props rule is §4.

## 1. Motif seeds and the three growth rules

**Derive the seed from the script's conflict (§1C), then grow it with the three rules (§1A).** The seed sets direction; the rules produce the garment.

The library in §1B is calibration, not a menu. A free choice between a fixed list and an undefined invention is always the list, and a fixed list gives two characters of one subgenre the same outfit. §1C replaces the choice with a procedure.

### 1A. Three growth rules

Answer all three before writing any garment line:

1. **Support — what carries it?** Which structure physically holds the seed: collar, yoke, bustle, hem, harness, sash. Also answer how it is built — the understructure, the attachment, the seam. A load-bearing garment with no visible support reads as a modelling error.
2. **Pressure — where does the stress land?** A metaphor under load marks one place: a hem, a shoulder, a seam, a knee. This becomes where the imperfection lives.
3. **Hands — what does it make the body do?** The hands may be empty, closed, hidden, braced, or touching the body; they never hold an object. A seed whose answer is "the hands grip X" needs rewriting, not a prop.

A seed that cannot answer all three is decoration. Change the seed.

**Register note**: editorial prefers event and behaviour seeds, because they imply a posture, a wear pattern, and a scar. Glamour prefers material seeds, because they imply a surface, a lustre, and a silhouette — and because a glamour character's biography is usually supplied by the script. Inversion seeds work in both.

### 1B. Seed library

**Calibration set.** Read these for the grammar — a seed is one short metaphor with a support, a pressure point, and a hand behaviour. Then derive your own with §1C. Use a library seed directly only when the brief has no character conflict to work from, and record that in the blueprint and the batch ledger so it is not spent twice.

Every seed is prop-free: the hands column describes what the body does, never what it carries.

### 1C. Deriving a seed from the script

The seed library in §1B is a **calibration set, not a menu.** Its job is to show what a usable seed looks like: one short metaphor, prop-free, answerable by the three growth rules. It is not a list to pick from, and it is not organised by genre on purpose — a genre-to-seed lookup table collapses 47 candidates into the two or three that fit a subgenre, which means every character of that subgenre gets the same outfit.

**Derive the seed from the character's conflict. Do this before opening §1B.**

1. **Name the conflict, not the role.** Not "a sect elder" but "she is enforcing a rule nobody else still keeps." Not "a ferryman" but "he carries people across a river he is not allowed to cross himself."
2. **Name what the conflict costs in the body's daily actions.** The rule-keeper spends her mornings straightening things nobody looks at. The ferryman never sits down. This is where the seed's pressure will land.
3. **Name the one object-class the conflict makes impossible or compulsory.** Something they must always wear, or can never wear. That class is the seed's support.
4. **Write the seed as a short metaphor in the same grammar as §1B** — a noun phrase, not a garment. If the candidate already names sleeves, filigree, and tassels it is an outfit, not a seed. Rewrite it.
5. **Run the three growth rules.** If any of the three cannot be answered, the seed is decoration — return to step 1 rather than reaching for the library.

**Worked derivation, so the step is not abstract.**

| Step | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Conflict | an archivist who has been told the archive is being demolished next month |
| Cost in daily action | she keeps arranging things that will be destroyed; her hands are always lifting and setting down |
| Compulsory or forbidden class | she must still wear the institution's formal collar, and it is the only formal thing left on her |
| Seed | **The Collar Kept for a Building** |
| Support | the collar itself, stiffened and re-stiffened, mounted on a plain worn dress |
| Pressure | the collar edge, where her throat has worn the stiffness down |
| Hands | lifting and setting down; one hand at the collar when she is told something she will not answer |

That seed appears in no table above and does not need to. It arrived from the script.

**When to use §1B instead:** when the brief supplies only a genre and no character conflict — a background figure, an unnamed guard, a design test. Then a library seed is the honest choice, and it should be labelled as library-sourced in the blueprint so the next character can avoid it. Check the batch ledger first; a library seed already spent on another character is out of circulation.

**Cluster discipline.** Two characters of one subgenre must not both take a library seed from the same family. §1B's family labels (Event / Behaviour / Inversion / Material) are the axis: if the previous character used Material, this one takes Event, Behaviour, or Inversion, or derives its own. This replaces the old genre routing table as the only narrowing rule.

## 2. The five gravity topologies and back-view continuity

Pick one topology per character. Never distribute detail evenly, and never reuse the same sentence order across characters. Topology governs where detail concentrates; silhouette family governs the outline. They are independent.

| Topology | Rule | Back view |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Top-Peak Cascade** | ~80% of complexity at crown, hair ornaments, collar, shoulders, or upper mantle; clean vertical lines below the chest | a high-collared mantle or waterfall of hair, ribbons, and crown chains pouring down an austere back drape |
| **Bottom-Anchored Weight** | upper body starkly minimalist or tightly wrapped; heavy drapery, brocade, or armour from hips to floor | exposed upper back leading into a massive rear waist-girdle or blooming multi-layered train |
| **Single-Side Mutation** | radical left-right contrast: one side tailored and plain, the other deconstructed, exposed, armoured, or overtaken by the motif | a bold diagonal architectural cut or asymmetrical cape exposing one side of the back |
| **Languid & Uncinched Negative Space** | no tight corsets or heavy boots; loose oversized drapery slipping off a shoulder, bare feet or soft shoes | loose billowing folds with hand-dyed tonal gradients, hair pinned with one rustic element |
| **Bone-Sculpted Tension** | strict enclosure (high collar, cinched waist, long leg line) against a deliberate structural cutout spanned by skeletal metalwork | a continuous vertical spinal axis connecting crown to a relief motif at the rear hem |

- Topology 1 is the easiest to render: the clean lower two-thirds gives cloth somewhere to show weight.
- Topology 2's lower mass needs a support story — a hoop, a layered horsehair braid, a plated armature.
- Topology 3 is **not** the `Asymmetric` silhouette family; a column silhouette can use Single-Side Mutation.
- Topology 4 is the hardest at physical fidelity: name where the cloth is caught, where it releases, and where it pools.
- Topology 5 pairs naturally with hourglass or inverted-triangle outlines, but the outline is chosen separately.
- Glamour most often resolves at Top-Peak Cascade, because the register's selling-point order puts the face and hair first. **That is a consequence of the register's priorities, not a rule.** Bottom-Anchored, Languid, Single-Side Mutation, and Bone-Sculpted are all legal in glamour; choosing one requires that the face and hair still win, and that compensation is the design work. A glamour cast where every character is Top-Peak has one silhouette system — the batch rule is in [../contract/gates.md](../contract/gates.md) §9, axis 3.
- Cloth volume needs a support story; head volume does not. Above the shoulders, volume may float freely.

## 3. The 70% triple-texture matrices

These implement the 70% primary hue only. The 25% anchor and 5% focal colour are separate decisions, defined in [form.md](form.md) §3. Weave the three textures into the garment pieces as you describe them; never append a separate colour-summary sentence.

| Family | Matte base | Tone-on-tone mid | Sheer / specular edge | Light behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Bone / chalk white** | matte raw ivory silk with visible slub | raised bone-on-bone floral jacquard whose weft catches a soft specular | multi-layered translucent bone-white organza with a clean rolled edge and hand-hammered silver relief | broad soft base, translucent edge, one tight specular in the relief |
| **Obsidian / blue-black** | heavy matte blue-black silk-linen with a dry hand | black-on-black embossed jacquard that appears only at grazing light | layered smoke-grey translucent gauze with brushed dark-gold hardware | broad soft base, translucent layer, tight specular in the metal; never plain `ink black` |
| **Celadon / mist jade** | muted slate-celadon matte crepe with a chalky surface | tonal woven bamboo damask with a raised weft | sheer pale-jade mist chiffon and carved jade clasps with internal clouding | broad soft base, translucent layer, tight specular in the jade |
| **Cinnabar / iron rust** | deep matte oxblood velvet with a directional pile | tone-on-tone cinnabar brocade with a dry, slightly stiff hand | gossamer flame-red silk organza hems and oxidized bronze filigree with green-black patina | volumetric base, translucent hem, rough specular in the patina |
| **Glacier / titanium grey** | matte ash-grey technical ripstop with a dry grid texture | tonal micro-pleated carbon-weave panels | iridescent frost-sheer film and brushed titanium with a fine directional grain | broad soft base, translucent film, tight specular in the titanium |

- **25% anchor tone**: hair mass, boots, belts, or structural metalwork. Name its temperature bias: `blue-black`, `warm iron black`, `brown-black`, `green-black`. A neutral "black" anchor is the most common cause of a grey, flat render.
- **5% focal colour**: irises, eye/lip micro-makeup, and one or two small motif cores only. One saturated accent per character.
- **Chromatic anchor**: one structural colour that is neither the 70% base nor the 5% iris accent, and that stays recognisable at thumbnail size — an azurite placket seam, a malachite cord, a realgar belt tab.
- Apply the token-frequency quota in [form.md](form.md) §3B.

The makeup column is also a skin column: every persona needs a different specular story and a different amount of visible texture. Full-body panels stay smooth; the texture tokens belong in the macro cell.

| Archetype | Eye and lip micro-makeup | Skin and hair | Bottom-right macro shift |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Aloof / divine-demonic** | ruby or amber glass irises with radial fibre, dusty-rose shadow around the lower lids, long wispy lashes, glossy berry lips, tiny tear-mole | cool porcelain with a matte cheek and a tight specular on the nose bridge; waist-length blue-black hair with silver-white streaks, airy forehead wisps, straight white silk tassels | one mouth corner lifts three millimetres and settles, lower lids tighten, eyes stay relaxed |
| **Imperial / ruthless / cold swordsman** | pale-gold or ice-grey irises with a hard catchlight, deep-set phoenix eyes with cool ash-taupe shadowing, sharp lower lashes, thin cool lips | controlled matte skin with a hard specular break across the cheekbone, one faint dry patch; long blue-black hair half-crowned in obsidian and dull gold, thin braided dark-gold chains | lower lids narrow, mouth stays flat, jaw sets, one brow lowers two millimetres |
| **Languid / world-weary / poetic immortal** | misty grey-amber irises, half-lidded gaze with sleepy peach-taupe lower-eyelid shading, soft pale matte lips with slight chapping | soft diffuse glow and no hard highlight, one small mark at the jaw; loosely unbound blue-black hair with windblown cheek wisps, a weathered branch pin | inner brows lift slightly, lips part two millimetres, a slow exhale, eyes stay half-lidded |
| **Fierce / unruly / martial prodigy** | glowing cinnabar irises, sharp upward-slanted brows, unadorned honeyed skin with a faint gold-leaf or scar mark across the nose bridge | sun-flushed skin with a real sweat sheen at the temple, a healed abrasion on one knuckle; messy short ash-white or charcoal hair with jagged wisps and a trailing red headband | one mouth corner pulls up, nose bridge wrinkles, eyes stay fixed |
| **Avant-garde / cool syndicate / cyber** | icy-cyan or molten-gold optical irises with a mechanical fibre pattern, razor-sharp smoky winged liner, matte burgundy or rose-taupe lips, micro-metallic cheek mark | even matte skin with a planned specular sweep, a faint micro-scar at the temple; sleek platinum bob or high wolf-cut with contrasting inner streaks | lower lids narrow, mouth flat, chin lifted five degrees, gaze fixed |
| **Labour / service / low-status grounded** | dull warm-brown or hazel irises with a hard catchlight, no visible eye makeup, chapped or pressed lips, one small occupational scar | sun-weathered skin with a dry reddened nostril edge and a real sweat sheen; hair flattened by a cap or tie, two centimetres too long, one strand escaping | one nostril flares slightly, mouth shut, eyes not moving |

Any of these can be re-gendered or age-shifted; the tokens are about temperament, not biology.

## 5A. Glamour hair system and selling-point order

In glamour register the hair is the first-layer system, not one dimension among ten.

| Layer | What to specify | Prompt tokens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mass and length** | total volume and where it ends | `waist-length`, `to the floor`, `ankle-grazing`, `a single glossy sheet` |
| **Crown volume** | height and shape at the top | `high crown`, `centre part`, `puffed at the crown`, `flattened by a band` |
| **Face framing** | how hair opens around the face | `side-swept curtains framing the cheeks`, `tucked behind one ear`, `a windblown strand across the brow` |
| **Texture behaviour** | how strands move and catch light | `flyaway strands catching the backlight`, `softly grouped locks`, `glossy`, `matted` |
| **Inner streaks** | the linear value interruption | `silver-white inner streaks`, `pale jade underlayer`, `cool-toned underlights` |
| **Headpiece inventory** | every piece, with hanging depth | `crown of silver starflowers`, `crystal drop tassels to the collarbone`, `one pin at the left temple` |

Rules:

- Flyaways are mandatory: `individual flyaway strands catching the light` prevents the helmet look.
- Sculpted strand clumps are mandatory: `grouped locks with sculpted strand clumps, a soft anime-style highlight band across the crown, no card-like clumping`. A single glossy sheet must still be built from clumps.
- Streaks carry the palette: when the costume is monochrome, the inner streak is the cheapest way to introduce the accent without spending accent budget on the garment.
- Count the headpiece pieces: name two to four; more than five reads as an inventory list and the render merges them into noise.
- The back view is a hair view: full fall, part, chain, and whatever hangs down the spine.

Selling-point order — design and describe in this order, and let budget follow it:

| Rank | Element | What the viewer is checking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Face** | Do I want to keep looking at this person |
| 2 | **Hair** | Is it expensive, long, abundant, moving |
| 3 | **Silhouette and leg line** | Is the figure desirable (slits, heels, boots, cinched waist) |
| 4 | **Chest / collar / hero ornament** | The one piece of jewellery that reads as status |
| 5 | **Garment volume and train** | Scale, luxe, movement |
| 6 | **Back view** | Hair + train + one spine detail |
| 7 | **Material detail** | Last — and only if the first six are right |

Never open with the fabric. Open with the face, then the hair. Density descends: head is loudest, chest second, waist third, hem quietest. Even distribution is the only disqualifying failure, not the total amount.

## 6. Ensemble cast contrast matrix

When designing multiple characters for one project, or proposing three options in Mode B, differentiate across eight axes:

| Axis | A (ethereal / sovereign) | B (ruthless / asymmetric) | C (unruly / grounded) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Silhouette | column or bell; flowing S-curves | asymmetric; acute triangles, high slits | trapeze or heavy trapezoid; compact upper frame |
| Key colour | bone white or celadon mist-jade | blue-black or glacier titanium-grey | cinnabar oxblood or raw earth-linen |
| Hair outline | waist-length blue-black with silver streaks | sleek platinum bob or high wolf-cut | messy short ash-white or charcoal |
| Signature item | crown of lotus buds or white silk tassels | cracked heirloom watch or geometric ear cuff | red fabric headband or patched knee guard |
| Material family | matte silk + raised jacquard + translucent organza | bonded wool + obsidian + gold seams | raw linen + worn leather + dull steel |
| Light behaviour | translucent and broad soft | tight specular against matte | all four behaviours present |
| Posture | weight on back leg, hands loose, chin level | weight on one hip, hands still, chin lifted | weight forward, shoulders squared, hand at the hip |
| Face / marking | pale cool skin, faint silver lotus mark | obsidian-fractured cheek with gold seams | sun-weathered skin, old blade scar across the nose bridge |

Rules:

- Any two characters must differ on at least four axes; silhouette and key colour are mandatory.
- Wide-shot test: at 10%, can you tell them apart by outline alone?
- Grayscale test: can you tell them apart by value and key colour alone?
- Thumbnail test: if two palettes collapse into the same mass, add a structural anchor colour, not saturation.
- If two characters share more than three axes, redesign one.

## 7. I2V adaptation (optional)

For image-to-video pipelines, the render target is a single full-body frame that survives motion, not a concept sheet.

| Element | Sheet version | I2V version | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hem | floor-sweeping train | knee-length split hem, front and back | a train clips through the floor and itself on turns |
| Hair | waist-length loose | high-tied ponytail or bound knot, short wisps | long hair collides with shoulders and arms |
| Sleeves | wide floor-sweeping | wrist-bound fitted cuff | wide sleeves fuse with the torso in motion |
| Hanging elements | silk tassels, long chains | removed or shortened above the collarbone | tassels shear and smear across frames |
| Skirt volume | multiple layered drapery | contoured panels with a fixed silhouette | free layers flap inconsistently |
| Held object | out of scope in the sheet | designed in the shot prompt | the sheet stays prop-free |
| Harness | none | body-contoured straps | gives the model a stable tracking reference |
| Skin | porcelain with macro-only pores | same, plus a locked specular direction | inconsistent highlights flicker |
| Prompt layout | seven-panel sheet | single full-body, front three-quarter | the sheet layout does not survive animation |

Preserve the motif seed, palette, identity block, and light behaviour across the swap. The I2V frame is a different render target, not a different character. See [../pipeline/asset-chain.md](../pipeline/asset-chain.md) for the production chain.

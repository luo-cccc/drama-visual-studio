# Bank: Boldness — Risk, Levers, Anti-Conservative Rules

The aesthetic system is conservative by design: one focal point, 70/25/5, one hero node plus three supporting, six classic silhouettes, and tasteful materials. It produces polished characters, not memorable ones. This file adds controlled risk without losing readability.

Risk levels and the lever cap are defined once in [../contract/gates.md](../contract/gates.md) §3, together with the house-style risk calibration. This file supplies the levers themselves, the palettes, the silhouettes, and the bidirectional detection that keeps a design from drifting to either extreme.

The detection tables here are **diagnostic**. The delivery gate is the preflight in [../pipeline/design.md](../pipeline/design.md) §6.

## 1. Ten boldness levers

Choose 2–5 according to gear.

1. **Proportion distortion**: oversized shoulders, tiny waist, elongated legs, oversized hands, compressed torso, towering collar, small head.
2. **Volume extreme**: giant sleeves, crinoline, balloon skirt, collapsed cocoon, horizontal breadth, trailing mass, architectural frame.
3. **Colour clash**: acid green + oxblood, sulfur yellow + lilac, ice blue + rust, neon pink + moss, black + pastel. Replace the 5% accent with a 10–15% "wrong" colour.
4. **Material transgression**: glass, resin, latex, mirrored fragments, moss, living vines, paper pulp, hair, feathers, inflated nylon, 3D-printed lattice, liquid metal, smoke, light, frost, wax, tar.
5. **Silhouette hybrid**: garment + object (sword case as a train, armour as a skirt, halo as a collar), deconstructed tailoring, garment-as-scaffold, dress-as-weapon.
6. **Scale shift**: tiny head, giant sleeves, miniature accessories, oversized ornament, jewellery scaled up or down, a normal object made monumental.
7. **Deconstruction**: exposed seams, cut-outs, torn layers, inside-out lining, unfinished hems, visible boning or structure, a garment falling apart.
8. **Repetition / accumulation**: a hundred hairpins, cascading blade fragments, layered collars, repeated motifs, a halo of identical objects.
9. **Inversion**: hard/soft, sacred/profane, armour/silk, winter/summer, masculine/feminine tailoring codes, luxury/utility.
10. **One absurd or alien element**: an impossible flower, a floating organ, a second shadow, a halo of broken blades, a train made of paper, a living tattoo, a blade grown through the shoulder as ornament.

Levers 5, 6, 8, and 10 stay a silhouette, a scale, or an accumulation effect — never a held object. Props are out of scope in every register per [../contract/gates.md](../contract/gates.md) §4; a lever that requires holding something is not available.

Levers at house-style fidelity: proportion distortion, material transgression, scale shift, and repetition are strengthened by the anime/BJD pillars; volume extreme still needs a named understructure when it is cloth; colour clash needs a value gap; deconstruction needs real fibre behaviour at the torn edge; silhouette hybrid must state what attaches the object; the absurd element needs a design reason, not a physics one.

## 2. Anti-conservative rules

- Break **one** rule from [material.md](material.md) §2 per design: two focal points, four colours, asymmetrical imbalance, a "wrong" material, an unreadable silhouette, a second hero node. At Avant-garde, you may break one rule category (for example, the entire restraint budget).
- Include **one element that shouldn't be there**: a colour from another genre, a material from another world, an accessory with no function, a proportion deliberately off.
- **Avoid the reflex palette** (`moon white / celadon / ink / cinnabar`) unless the brief demands it. It is the most-reached-for combination in the genre, not the correct one — but it is a legitimate choice: in glamour register it is the category's native language. The test is whether you chose it or defaulted to it.
- Avoid the default silhouette (`column / bell`) unless the character demands it.
- The design must be describable in **one surprising sentence**, not just "beautiful".
- Risk goes into silhouette, material, colour, or one worn accessory. The face still wins.
- Two or three levers at gear 2, three or four at gear 3, at most five at gear 4. The risk must serve the character truth or seed, never random decoration.

## 3. Bidirectional detection

The rest of this file pushes upward. Without a counterweight, every design drifts to maximum ornament. Run the under-shoot check first, then the over-shoot table for the locked register.

### Under-shoot detection — run first

Over-shoot detection catches excess. It does not catch **plainness**, which is the more common failure and the one no cap can see.

| Measure | Minimum | Under-minimum action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Visible layers** | editorial working 2–3 · editorial high-status 4–5 · glamour 5–6 · ceremonial 6 | add the floating layer first, then the translucent outer layer |
| **Edge treatment rate** | 60% of the enumerated edges | apply one trim language across several edges in one clause |
| **Pattern coverage** | banded, at minimum, for any glamour or high-status figure | work one motif family tone-on-tone across one large field |
| **Tight specular** | at least one metal, jade, or polished edge | add a metal edge band — the cheapest place to buy "expensive" |
| **Ornament nodes** | editorial 4 · glamour 8–15 · ceremonial 15+ | add nodes at junctions, descending in scale |
| **Floating element** | at least one for glamour and any ceremonial figure | a sash, veil, or tassel hanging free |

Diagnostic phrase: if the one-sentence description is "X wears a [colour] [garment]", it is under-designed regardless of how many levers it uses. Richness lives in the count of construction decisions, not the count of adjectives.

### Over-shoot — editorial criteria

| Measure | Cap | Over-cap action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Levers used** | gear 2: 3 · gear 3: 4 · gear 4: 5 | cut to the cap, keeping the lever that serves the contradiction |
| **Primary ornament nodes** | 4 (one hero + three supporting) | delete the weakest node; do not merge two into one |
| **Occurrences of one colour term** | accent 2 · anchor 4 · base 8 | cut to the role's cap, keeping the accent on the focal point |
| **Occurrences of one material term** | 5 | collapse repeats into one compound noun |
| **Named fabric/material types** | 4 | remove the least load-bearing one |
| **Sentence-level metaphor clauses** | 1 | keep the thesis metaphor; convert the rest to physical description |
| **Render vocabulary repeats** | 1 per concept | see [form.md](form.md) §3C |

Signs a design is over the line even if counts look acceptable:

- The one-sentence description needs a comma-spliced list of three ideas.
- Two different things are both described as the focal point.
- The back view was described by adding more ornament rather than resolving the front's density.
- Removing any single ornament would not change the character.
- Render tokens do the work the character should be doing: `game CG, BJD, anime, 8k, ultra detailed` and still cannot say what the person does for a living.

### Over-shoot — glamour criteria

Glamour abundance is the product, so the measure changes from how much to how it is distributed. Count nothing by total; measure the gradient.

| Measure | Criterion | Fail action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ornament count** | no cap | none — count is not a defect in this register |
| **Density gradient** | head > chest > waist > hem, each strictly lighter than the one above | move ornament upward; never add more below to even it out |
| **Top-zone richness** | the head carries at least two distinct ornament groups | add one head group before adding anything lower |
| **Lower-half quiet area** | at least one plain run of fabric or bare skin below the waist | clear one zone; a fully patterned skirt reads as a solid block |
| **Accent colour cap** | 2 — still binding | cut to 2; the one editorial rule that survives intact |
| **Base vs accent ratio** | base colour terms outnumber the accent by at least 2:1 | if the accent is crowding the base, the palette has lost its hierarchy |
| **Specular highlight near the face** | one glossed element within the head/chest zone | add a glossed lip, wet lower lid, or polished metal near the cheek |
| **Chromatic anchor** | one structural colour outside the accent, legible after desaturation | add an azurite seam, a realgar cord, a cinnabar placket |
| **Even distribution** | any of the four zones matching its neighbour in density | redistribute upward — the only disqualifying failure |

What does not transfer from editorial: the remove-one test, the four-node cap, the named-material cap, and the one-hero-ornament requirement (hair may be the hero, and there may be several peers).

What stays identical in both registers: the lever cap by gear, the accent token cap, the face-wins rule, the no-props rule, and the ban on fluff adjectives.

## 4. Bold palettes

Every palette below is written as pigments, so it reads as designed rather than defaulted. Read each name as a mineral or oxide colour kept a step below the face's chroma, and add one counter-temperature material.

- **Acid Xianxia**: sulfur yellow, acid green, lampblack, one jade.
- **Oxidized**: verdigris, copper, blood orange, ash.
- **Ice + Rust**: pale ice blue, iron rust, charcoal, silver.
- **Neon Monastic**: neon pink, moss green, bone white, black.
- **Decadent**: absinthe green, bruised purple, antique gold, black.
- **Clinical**: bone white, brushed chrome, pale blue, hazard orange.
- **Toxic Pastel**: mint, lilac, warning orange, black.
- **Blood + Milk**: oxblood, warm ivory, black, silver.

Clash pairs need a value gap. Two clash colours at the same value merge into one grey under diffuse light. Choose a pair with at least two stops between them — sulfur yellow against lampblack works; sulfur yellow against bone white does not.

## 5. Bold materials

Glass, resin, latex, mirrored fragments, moss, living vines, paper pulp, human hair, feathers, inflated nylon, 3D-printed lattice, liquid metal, smoke, light, frost, wax, tar, oxidized copper, bioplastic, holographic film, shattered ceramics, bone, shell, coral, crystal, dust.

Each must be given a light behaviour: which of the four in [material.md](material.md) §1 it shows, and what it does to light at the edge. `moss` alone renders as a green patch; `moss with a damp matte surface and fine spore haze` renders as crafted moss. The BJD finish turns an impossible material from a render accident into a designed object.

## 6. Bold silhouettes

- **Horizontal breadth**: shoulder-to-shoulder width, wide sleeves, wide hem.
- **Giant cocoon**: body swallowed by volume.
- **Architectural frame**: collar, backpiece, or train as a structure.
- **Body extension**: sleeves longer than arms, train longer than body, hair as a cape.
- **Dress-as-armor**: skirt built from lacquered plates, sleeves as segmented guards (worn, never carried).
- **Deconstructed column**: a column that is cut, split, or falling apart.
- **Hoop or crinoline**: historical volume in xianxia materials.
- **Inverted triangle**: huge shoulders, tiny hips.
- **Tapered column**: tiny shoulders, massive hem.
- **Two-body silhouette**: a second volume behind or around the body.

Cloth volume still needs a support story: a bamboo hoop, a lacquered frame, a whalebone-like rib, a layered horsehair braid. At the head and shoulders, volume may float — anime readability makes an unsupported crown or levitating halo read as stylization.

## 7. Risk audit

1. Can you describe the design in one surprising sentence?
2. Which rule did you break?
3. Which two or three levers did you use — and are you sure it is not four or six?
4. Is there one element that shouldn't be there?
5. Is the palette something you have seen before?
6. Does the face still win?
7. Does it read at 10% size?
8. Is the risk in the design, not in the render?
9. **Count construction, not adjectives.** Can you name the layers, treated edges, nodes, and pattern coverage? If not, the design is plain and no lever will save it.
10. Is any extreme element at the head or silhouette spent freely, while any extreme cloth still names a support and a light behaviour?

If every answer is yes and the design still feels ordinary, the problem is not the risk level — it is that the seed is a material state rather than an event or a behaviour. A bolder material on a weak seed produces a louder average.

If the design is only beautiful and coherent, it is still Polished. Raise the level — but raise the concept, not the count.

If the design is tasteful and restrained but nobody would screenshot it, the problem is neither risk nor seed — it is density. Run the under-shoot table before raising the gear; density is the cheaper fix.

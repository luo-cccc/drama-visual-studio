# Engine 03: Costume Derivation, IFV, and the Five Craft Operators

Layers 2–5 of the derivation stack. This file turns the character's conflict into a **motif seed**, locks the **IFV triple**, and applies **five inline aesthetic & craft operators** (Proportion, Rhythm, Notan, Colour, Grounding) plus controlled boldness levers.

There is no seed menu and no preset outfit table here. Coordinate systems live in [../atlas/coordinates.md](../atlas/coordinates.md), density bands in [../atlas/density.md](../atlas/density.md), unbundled physical terms in [../atlas/lexicon.md](../atlas/lexicon.md), and genre cliché blacklists in [../atlas/genre-traps.md](../atlas/genre-traps.md).

## 1. Conflict-to-Seed Compiler & the IFV Triple

### 1.1 Derive the seed from the script's conflict (5 steps)

A motif seed is **one short physical metaphor** (a noun phrase under pressure, never a completed outfit). If a candidate already names sleeves, filigree, and tassels, it is a purchased costume — discard it.

1. **Name the conflict, not the role.** Not "a sect elder" but "she is enforcing a burial law nobody else still keeps." Not "a bodyguard" but "he is paid by the person he was sent to watch."
2. **Name what the conflict costs in the body's daily actions.** Where does friction, weight, or repetition hit the body every morning?
3. **Name the one garment or object-class the conflict makes compulsory or forbidden.** Something they can never take off, or are barred from wearing.
4. **Compress steps 1–3 into a 4-to-7-word physical metaphor** — e.g. `The Collar Kept for a Demolished Hall`, `A Coat Weighted with Unclaimed Tallies`, `Silk Re-sewn Over Splinted Bone`.
5. **Run the three growth rules (§1.2).** If any of the three cannot be answered with a physical noun, the seed is empty poetry — rewrite it.

### 1.2 The three growth rules (mandatory for every seed)

Answer all three in blueprint point 3 before writing a single garment clause:

| Rule | Question | What it produces in the prompt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Support** | Which load-bearing garment structure physically holds the seed, and how is it built? | collar, yoke, bustle armature, corset stay, harness, belt — plus its understructure, seam, or fastening |
| **2. Pressure** | Where does the mechanical stress or daily wear land on the body? | the exact boundary where the imperfection, repair, or contradiction signal sits (throat edge, left shoulder, knee, cuff) |
| **3. Empty hands** | What does this structure make the character's empty hands do? | connects directly to the pose vector ([01-truth-and-pose.md](01-truth-and-pose.md) §7); **never a held prop** |

**Two pure derivation traces (showing the mechanism, never to be reused as templates):**

- *Trace 1 (Editorial)*: Conflict = an archivist told her hall will be demolished next month → Daily cost = lifting and re-stacking wooden slips → Compulsory class = the stiff institutional neck-yoke → Seed = **`The Stiff Collar Outliving Its Archive`** → Support = a triple-starched linen standing collar mounted on an unbleached worn robe → Pressure = the raw upper collar rim where her jaw has rubbed the starch away → Hands = right fingers checking the collar clasp, left arm hanging still.
- *Trace 2 (Glamour)*: Conflict = a clan matriarch whose bloodline oath literally cools her extremities → Daily cost = hiding frostbite stiffness behind ceremonial stillness → Compulsory class = heated bronze filigree arm-cages under sheer silk → Seed = **`Frost Contained in Warm Bronze`** → Support = articulated dark-bronze spinal and cuff armatures beneath translucent smoky gauze → Pressure = the wrist and throat junctions where frost rime meets warm metal → Hands = both hands resting open against the warm bronze waist-girdle.

### 1.3 Lock the IFV triple (Invariant / Free / Forbidden)

Immediately after deriving the seed, check [../atlas/genre-traps.md](../atlas/genre-traps.md) for the locked subgenre and declare:

- **Invariant (1 item):** The one structural rule from Support/Pressure that cannot be compromised (e.g. `the high starched neck-yoke stays closed and rigid in every view`).
- **Free (2–3 items):** Which garment zones are open for aesthetic exploration in §2 (e.g. `lower skirt pleat geometry, sleeve width below the elbow, back-sash termination`).
- **Forbidden (3 items):** Three specific genre reflexes from `genre-traps.md` banned from this character (e.g. `no floating shoulder ribbons, no lotus hair crown, no moon-white + silver-filigree default`).

## 2. The Five Inline Craft Operators

Counts alone make a design legal; these five operators make it visually compelling. Apply each operator directly when building blueprint points 4, 5, and 6.

### 2.1 Operator: Proportion & Cut (`craft: proportion`)

**Question:** *Why does one silhouette look authoritative or expensive, while another looks like a cheap sack?*

A head-count number (`8.5-head`) means nothing if the garment cuts the body in halves. Every costume must declare **three vertical cut points** and avoid equal 1:1 divisions:

1. **Head-count & Torso attitude:**
   - `8.5-head`: reads as **distance, untouchability, austerity, or high fashion**. Costs approachability.
   - `7.5-to-8-head`: reads as **physical weight, martial readiness, grounded humanity**.
   - `7.0-to-7.5-head`: reads as **youth, compression, or coiled energy**.
2. **Garment waist-cut vs anatomical waist (Root-Rectangle Split ~ 1 : 1.414 or Golden ~ 3 : 5):**
   - **High cut (under-bust / Empire, 2:5 upper-to-lower)**: lengthens legs dramatically, compresses the ribcage; reads ceremonial, archaic, or towering.
   - **Natural anatomical waist with hard cinch**: emphasizes hourglass tension and shoulder-to-hip ratio.
   - **Dropped / low-hip cut (5:3 upper-to-lower)**: lengthens the torso, lowers the centre of gravity; reads heavy, martial, or languid.
   - **Uncinched continuous drop**: erases the waist; requires a strong shoulder break or hem flare so it does not read as a cylinder.
3. **Hem & Sleeve termination (never land on a joint by accident):**
   - **Never cut a hem or cuff dead on the knee joint or wrist bone** unless stiffness is the point; land **above the knee, mid-calf ( widest/narrowest contrast), 3cm above the ankle bone, or pooling on the floor**.
   - **Sleeve vs hand:** bare wrist (working/martial readiness), half-palm coverage (guarded elegance), or floor-length over-sleeve (ceremonial immobilization — must state where the inner fitted cuff peeks out).

### 2.2 Operator: Rhythm, Hierarchy & The Breakpoint (`craft: rhythm`)

**Question:** *Why does one ornate outfit read as music, while another reads as spare parts glued to a mannequin?*

1. **The Rule of Three & Scale Gap:** Never place four equal-sized ornaments or four equal-width bands in a row — four equal items read as a fence. Use **1 dominant hero + 2 or 3 clearly subordinate supports**, where the subordinate elements are **at most one-third the visual area** of the hero.
2. **Progressive interval (递进):** Layer hems and collar bands must step in **unequal intervals** (e.g. a 2cm inner collar line, an 8cm mid-yoke, a 25cm outer lapel drop). Equal spacing reads as mechanical stripes.
3. **The Mandatory Breakpoint (一处破律):** A rhythmic system only looks alive when **one spot deliberately breaks the pattern**:
   - In a strictly symmetrical gown: one asymmetrical side-slit, one missing cuff clasp, or a single diagonal chain crossing the chest.
   - In an allover patterned field: **one primary open area (留白呼吸区)** of completely unadorned fabric or bare skin (e.g. an unpatterned dark wool chest panel, bare collarbones, or a plain matte lower sleeve) that gives the eye somewhere to rest.

### 2.3 Operator: Notan — 10% Value Masses (`craft: notan`)

**Question:** *If I squint or shrink the sheet to 10% grayscale, can I still tell who this is and where the face sits?*

Before picking any hue, assign the character's silhouette into **three unequal grayscale value blocks**:

1. **Area allocation (70 / 25 / 5 in value, not just hue):**
   - **Dominant mass (~65–70%)**: either Light (`#EAE6DF` to `#F4F1EA`), Mid-value (`#6E6A64` to `#8C857B`), or Dark (`#1A1C20` to `#282423`). **Do not default every character to a Light gown + Dark hair.**
   - **Counter-value anchor (~20–25%)**: the opposing value block that carves the silhouette (e.g. dark boots/corset/hair against a light robe, or a pale bone-white shoulder mantle against an obsidian coat).
   - **Bridge midtone (~5–10%)**: prevents the two masses from looking like a sticker cutout.
2. **Focal contrast lock:** The **highest dark-against-light contrast edge on the entire sheet must sit at the face, throat, or chest** — never at the hem or boots. If a dark-haired character wears a dark high collar, open a pale skin v-neck or insert a pale inner collar rim so the head does not merge into the torso in grayscale.
3. **Anti-Mud Guard:** If more than 50% of the figure sits in mid-grey without a deep dark anchor or a clean light block, the 3D render will look washed out. Push either the anchor down to `#1E1E22` or the light mass up to `#F2EFE8`.

### 2.4 Operator: Colour Architecture & Temperature Clash (`craft: colour`)

**Question:** *Why do some palettes look like high-end animation key art, while others look like cheap plastic dye?*

1. **Chroma hierarchy (Face > Chromatic Anchor > Garment Base):**
   - Cheapness comes from **high chroma at large area**. The 70% garment base must sit at **low-to-medium chroma** (mineral, earth, ash, bone, slate, oxblood-dark), so the **5% iris/lip/focal accent** and the **structural chromatic anchor** pop without screaming.
2. **Mandatory Temperature Clash (冷暖对抗):**
   - A warm-dominant palette (ochre, rust, warm ivory, bronze) **must** carry one cold structural interrupt (verdigris patina, slate-celadon cord, cool iron-black).
   - A cool-dominant palette (glacier grey, blue-black, celadon, silver) **must** carry one warm spark (burnt amber, cinnabar seal, raw brass, warm bone).
3. **Triple-Texture Split within the 70% Base:**
   - Never apply one flat colour across the 70% mass. Split the primary hue into **three light behaviours** using unbundled terms from [../atlas/lexicon.md](../atlas/lexicon.md) §1–2:
     - **Matte structural core** (absorbs light, shows weave/nap)
     - **Tone-on-tone mid-layer** (jacquard, pleat, embossing — catches grazing light only)
     - **Sheer or tight-specular boundary** (organza veil, lacquered edge, hammered metal rim)
4. **Token-Frequency Discipline:** Enforce [../atlas/lexicon.md](../atlas/lexicon.md) §5 caps (`accent ≤ 2, anchor ≤ 4, base ≤ 8, single material ≤ 5`).

### 2.5 Operator: Physical & Historical Grounding (`craft: grounding`)

**Question:** *Why does this look like a real garment built by a tailor or armourer, rather than AI prompt soup?*

1. **Every volume needs a load-bearing story:**
   - If a collar stands 8cm high, state what holds it up (`stiffened with interlined hemp`, `supported by a bronze throat-ring`).
   - If a skirt bells out or a cape holds an architectural fold, name the support (`quilted horsehair hem pad`, `split bamboo stays`, `heavy tarred canvas weight`).
   - If an armour plate or heavy chest ornament sits on silk, name the attachment (`riveted to a leather under-harness`, `suspended from a braided shoulder cord`, `pierced through a reinforced placket`). Unattached metal floating on soft cloth reads as a rendering glitch.
2. **Cultural & structural specificity (Kill "Pan-Oriental" vagueness):**
   - Never write `ancient Chinese robe` or `oriental dress`. Name the exact construction mechanics from [../atlas/lexicon.md](../atlas/lexicon.md) §3: `y-shaped cross-collar (jiaoling) tied at the right rib`, `high-waisted pleated overskirt with a flat front apron (mamian construction)`, `round-neck robe (yuanling) buttoned at the right clavicle with a split side vent`, `double-breasted 1930s storm-flap trench`.
3. **Gravity and friction traces:**
   - Cloth pulls downward from its pinning points: name where the folds **gather** (at the belt cinch, over the forearm crook) and where they **release**.

## 3. Boldness Levers, Glamour Hair, and I2V Adaptation

### 3.1 The ten boldness levers (means, not preset answers)

Select **2–3 levers at gear 2 Signature**, **3–4 at gear 3 Avant-garde**, **up to 5 at gear 4 Surreal** ([../contract/gates.md](../contract/gates.md) §3). Every chosen lever must serve the motif seed (§1.1) or contradiction (§5 in Engine 01), and you must state **which one restraint rule was deliberately broken**:

1. **Proportion distortion**: towering collar, compressed ribcage, floor-dragging sleeves, extreme shoulder yoke breadth.
2. **Volume extreme**: architectural train, collapsed cocoon mass, wide stiffened skirt frame (must name understructure per Operator 2.5).
3. **Colour clash**: replace the 5% accent with a 10–15% high-contrast clash colour separated by at least two value stops (e.g. sulfur-gold against lampblack; verdigris against deep oxblood).
4. **Material transgression**: introduce one material from outside tailoring — volcanic glass, raw paper pulp, dried moss, liquid mercury, tarred rope, ceramic shards, bone splints — with its exact light behaviour.
5. **Silhouette hybrid**: fuse two incompatible garment classes (armour tassets tailored as a pleated skirt, mourning veil built like a cage, surgical apron over court brocade).
6. **Scale shift**: monumentally oversized single clasp or hairpin against micro-fine chainwork; zero medium-sized ornaments.
7. **Deconstruction**: exposed structural boning, inside-out basting stitches, raw heat-fused hems, slashed outer shell revealing the inner lining.
8. **Repetition / accumulation**: a dense cluster of 20+ identical tiny functional items in one zone (e.g. brass tally tags along a shoulder strap, bone needles pinned in a row along a lapel).
9. **Code inversion**: hard industrial hardware on feather-light gauze; sacred ordination vestments tailored like a butcher's apron.
10. **One impossible / alien anchor**: a crown element suspended 2cm above the hair without contact, a seam that leaks pale ash, a shadow that does not match the collar cut (spend above the shoulders so cloth physics below stay grounded).

### 3.2 Glamour hair system & selling-point order

In `glamour` register, hair is the second-highest selling point right behind the face. Specify all **six layers** when writing a glamour character:

| Layer | What to decide | Structural tokens (combine with house clump craft) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Mass & length** | total silhouette volume and termination point | `ankle-grazing`, `waist-length`, `cropped asymmetrical bob`, `high-bound cascading tail` |
| **2. Crown architecture** | top silhouette height and parting | `high-puffed crown`, `razor-straight centre part`, `swept back under a tight band` |
| **3. Face framing** | how locks cut across the cheek and jaw | `side-swept curtains narrowing the cheek`, `tucked cleanly behind one ear`, `jagged temple locks` |
| **4. Strand physics** | mandatory house craft + motion | `grouped locks with sculpted strand clumps, a soft anime-style highlight band across the crown, individual flyaway strands catching the backlight` |
| **5. Value interruption** | linear contrast inside the hair mass | `dyed underlayer streaks`, `braided metallic wire`, `pale ash-white temple lock` |
| **6. Headpiece inventory** | 2–4 distinct pieces with hanging depth (5+ merges into noise) | state exact zone and drop depth (e.g. `temple pin dropping a chain to the jaw angle`) |

**Glamour selling-point priority (allocate prompt detail in this order):**
`1. Face (§1A+§1B) → 2. Hair (6 layers) → 3. Silhouette & leg/waist cut → 4. Hero throat/chest node → 5. Garment layers & train → 6. Back-view spine resolution → 7. Micro-weave texture.`

### 3.3 I2V (Image-to-Video) adaptation rules

When the target is a single full-body frame for video generation rather than a 7-panel concept sheet:

- Shorten floor-sweeping trains to **knee- or calf-length split hems** (prevents floor clipping on turns).
- Bind waist-length loose hair into a **high ponytail, braided coil, or structured knot** (prevents shoulder/arm collision).
- Replace wide hanging sleeves with **wrist-bound cuffs or forearm wraps**.
- Shorten hanging tassels/chains to **above the collarbone**, and add **body-contoured belts/straps** to give the video model stable tracking anchors.
